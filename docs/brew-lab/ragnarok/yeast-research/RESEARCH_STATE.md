# Ragnarok yeast research — durable R1–R6 state

Date: 2026-09-13

Status: **Discovery/research advanced; final yeast decision not yet made.**

This file preserves the decision-relevant research state in normal Git text. It is not Core masterdata.

## 1. Research question

Find both:

1. the **best single yeast** for Kvernhaug Ragnarok, and
2. the **best multi-yeast strategy**, which may use a flavor-primary plus a helper/finisher with a deliberately narrow job.

The combo winner does not have to contain the best solo yeast. A strain that is technically weak alone can be valuable if it contributes exceptional flavor/body and a second strain solves the exact missing fermentation task.

## 2. Ragnarok sensory brief

Ragnarok is intended as an intensely flavor-dense imperial stout, not a pastry stout and not an adjunct showcase. Desired direction:

- enormous but integrated dark chocolate / roast / espresso-like malt character
- dark caramel / toffee / Demerara
- potentially dark fruit from yeast: plum, raisin, fig, dark cherry
- dense, long finish and substantial body without becoming syrup
- warm alcohol is acceptable if integrated
- possible subtle smoke as Kvernhaug signature, but not a smoked-stout identity
- avoid obvious orange/citrus, banana, bubblegum and sharp phenolic/spice character unless later evidence changes the brief

Working philosophy: **“smaken bestemmer styrken.”** Roughly 13–16% ABV is acceptable; a great 13% beer beats a poor 16% beer.

## 3. Evidence hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. **Producer technical documentation** — highest authority for strain properties.
2. Producer product pages / technical PDFs / TDS.
3. Technical/professional brewing sources.
4. Brewer/community case studies — observations, not general truth.
5. Kvernhaug Brew Lab batch observations — observations, not general truth.
6. Our interpretation for Ragnarok.
7. Hypotheses that require testing.

If a retailer and producer disagree on a technical property, producer data wins unless stronger primary evidence is found. Retailers are mainly used for current availability/pack-size data.

### R3 solo/fatal-flaw dispositions

- **LalBrew Nottingham — KEEP.** No clear fatal flaw. Main caution: producer ceiling ~14%; can attenuate aggressively depending on wort/process.
- **Omega Dried Lutra — KEEP / clean option.** 14–16%, neutral; weakness is minimal positive flavor contribution.
- **LalBrew House Ale — KEEP.** Very robust/neutral; 14% ceiling and 78–86% attenuation can run dry.
- **WHC British Pub Ale — KEEP.** 14%, 76–80%, English/Imperial-Stout-compatible character; weak current Norwegian small-pack availability.
- **WHC High Voltage — KEEP / technical dark horse.** 17%, 76–80%, remarkably clean; very high nitrogen demand, 31–35°C recommended, pack availability awkward.
- **LalBrew BRY-97 — KEEP / lower ceiling.** 13% documented tolerance.
- **WHC Old English — KEEP / dark horse.** 14%, low ester, low nitrogen demand, high-gravity recommendation.
- **WLP518 Opshaug Kveik — KEEP / quiet dark horse.** 15%+, 69–80%, STA1-negative; flavor temperature dependence remains a concern.
- **WLP099 Super High Gravity — specialist/backup.** Huge alcohol ceiling, but STA1+, 80–100% attenuation and process/body risk.
- **WLP545 Belgian Strong — deprioritized as primary.** Strong technical ceiling but STA1+, high attenuation, black pepper/clove/herbal/banana conflict.
- **K.9 Ebbegarden / Hornindal / K.14 Eitrheim — deprioritized as primary** because tropical/citrus/orange direction conflicts with current brief (plus Eitrheim's lower ceiling).
- **CellarScience JUNGLE — deprioritized as primary** due strong guava/passionfruit/mango profile despite excellent technical capability.
- **HA-18 / DW-17 — specialist only** because glucoamylase can attack the residual-body target.
- **WLP004 Irish Ale — drop as solo, retain as combo flavor-primary.** Excellent stout character; technical alcohol/attenuation ceiling too low alone.

## 7. R4–R5 — helper-yeast concept

A helper is not automatically a second primary yeast. It must have a defined job.

Architectures under consideration:

- single primary (benchmark)
- flavor primary → neutral beer-wort finisher
- flavor primary → robust high-gravity beer finisher
- flavor primary → late simple-sugar/high-alcohol helper
- two character-positive strains with different jobs
- planned high-ABV helper
- emergency rescue only
- enzyme-assisted extreme helper (specialist branch)

### Pair evaluation criteria

- flavor ownership preserved — 25%
- completion gain — 20%
- FG/body protection — 20%
- late-pitch viability — 15%
- KISS / process burden — 8%
- predictability — 7%
- contamination/future-batch safety — 5%

Again, weighted pair scores are structured hypotheses, not measured facts.

### Role-map highlights

- **AEB Bel-Abbey:** strong flavor primary; dried fruit + caramel; ~12% ceiling.
- **WLP004 Irish Ale:** strong stout flavor primary/body role; weak solo ceiling.
- **M15 Empire Ale:** full body/dark fruit; only ~8% ceiling; killer-sensitive — interesting precisely because a helper could solve its weakness.
- **WLP530 Abbey Ale:** cherry/plum/pear flavor-primary candidate.
- **WLP013 London Ale:** oak/light stone fruit/biscuit complexity; flavor-primary candidate.
- **Nottingham:** excellent solo candidate and credible beer-wort finisher.
- **British Pub Ale:** solo candidate and character-positive helper.
- **Lutra:** preferred neutral high-tolerance beer helper candidate.
- **High Voltage:** technical high-ABV helper/solo candidate, but warm/high-nitrogen process.
- **WLP099:** extreme finisher/rescue specialist.
- **EC-1118:** simple-sugar/high-alcohol helper candidate only; do not assume it can finish residual beer sugars.

### R5 combo architecture leaders

- **Neutral wort finisher:** Bel-Abbey → Lutra
- **Robust beer finisher:** Bel-Abbey → Nottingham
- **Late simple-sugar finisher:** Bel-Abbey → EC-1118, only if EC-1118's job is late Demerara/simple sugar rather than residual maltotriose
- **Dual-positive character:** Bel-Abbey → British Pub Ale
- **Classic stout primary + helper:** WLP004 → Lutra / Nottingham
- **Body/dark-fruit primary + helper:** M15 → Lutra (high-risk/high-reward)
- **Thermotolerant extreme helper:** Bel-Abbey → High Voltage
- **Extreme finisher:** Bel-Abbey → WLP099 (specialist only)

## 8. R6 — brewer/community observations

Community evidence is secondary to producer data. It was used to increase/decrease confidence and expose failure modes.

### Nottingham

Relevant observations found:

- 38P imperial-stout wort reportedly reached 13P without unusual tricks.
- 1.112 → 1.027 reported with ~20 g yeast in 12 L.
- 1.102 → 1.019 reported with two packs and very vigorous fermentation.
- 26P RIS reportedly fermented through in four days.
- Counterexample: 1.102 stout stalled around 1.048 despite a large pitch.
- Counterexample: 1.100 stout with simple sugar reportedly finished around 1.005 after long aging.

Interpretation: high confidence that Nottingham is genuinely capable in big stout, but wort fermentability and process can swing final gravity widely.

### WHC British Pub Ale

Producer-hosted customer report: 1.100 imperial stout fermented quickly, flocculated well and produced a complex/multilayered beer.

Interpretation: strengthens it as a flavor-forward solo finalist, but similar-case evidence is still much smaller than Nottingham's.

### Omega Lutra

Community users report good results in big stouts and very clean fermentation in dark beer.

Interpretation: strengthens the neutral-helper hypothesis, while exact sequential-pitch behavior remains unproven.

### WLP004 Irish Ale

Imperial-stout reports show vigorous early fermentation and comparatively high final gravity / early settling.

Interpretation: reinforces its proposed role as a **flavor primary** rather than solo Ragnarok yeast.

### M15 → Nottingham

Community discussions explicitly propose the same architecture: character yeast first, Nottingham for attenuation, with only a short head start rather than waiting for a dead stall. A shared RIS recipe uses both M15 and Nottingham.

Interpretation: combo is no longer purely theoretical, but timing/killer compatibility still requires controlled validation.

### EC-1118

Reports are conflicting as a stuck-beer rescue. Experienced brewers repeatedly caution that wine yeast may not solve residual maltose/maltotriose problems. A barleywine example using beer yeast first and EC-1118 with later candi sugar did show renewed fermentation; other already-high-ABV applications have failed to establish.

Interpretation: **role narrowed** to staged simple sugar / high-alcohol duty. Do not call it a generic beer finisher.

### AEB Bel-Abbey

Producer flavor/technical evidence remains attractive, but this pass did not find a strong independent imperial-stout/high-gravity outcome case.

Interpretation: advance as flavor-primary candidate, but flag for controlled split-batch validation.

## 10. Important compatibility/process warnings

- Alcohol tolerance on a datasheet is **not** proof a helper can establish after alcohol is already high and nutrients/easy sugars are depleted.
- Prefer planned helper addition while primary fermentation is still active; true rescue is a different protocol.
- M15 is documented as killer-sensitive; helper compatibility must be checked before locking any M15 pair.
- EC-1118 is not assumed to consume residual maltotriose/complex beer sugars.
- WLP099 is STA1-positive and highly attenuative; body and sanitation strategy are mandatory if used.
- HA-18/DW-17 contain glucoamylase and therefore change fermentability rather than acting as neutral helpers.
- High Voltage's 31–35°C recommendation and very high nitrogen demand make a sequential handoff non-trivial.

## 11. What still prevents a final choice

The major missing variable is **the actual fermentation task**.

Before selecting solo vs combo, Ragnarok V1 needs a concrete wort model containing at least:

- target starting OG / gravity at primary pitch
- desired FG / residual-body target
- realistic desired ABV window
- proportion of fermentables from malt vs Demerara
- whether Demerara is in the kettle or staged later
- if staged: addition timing / gravity / estimated ABV when added
- mash strategy and expected wort fermentability
- which sugars are likely to remain when the primary strain starts to become stressed
- temperature / oxygen / nutrient plan

Why this matters:

- If the primary beer yeast has already consumed the relevant maltose/maltotriose and late gravity comes mainly from added Demerara, EC-1118-like simple-sugar help may be elegant.
- If significant maltose/maltotriose remains when the primary weakens, the helper must be a true beer-wort finisher such as Lutra/Nottingham/another suitable beer strain.
- The best solo yeast can only be judged fairly once all solo candidates are asked to ferment the same defined wort.

**Next research/design step: build Ragnarok V1 fermentation model, then run every serious solo and combo finalist against that same task.**
